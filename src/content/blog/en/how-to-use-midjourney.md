# How to Use Midjourney: Complete Beginner's Guide

Midjourney has become one of the most popular AI image generation tools available today, and for good reason. It produces stunning, artistic images that rival professional digital artwork, and it does so from simple text descriptions. Whether you want to create concept art for a game, illustrations for a blog post, marketing visuals for social media, or simply explore your creative ideas, Midjourney makes it accessible to everyone — no artistic skills required.

This complete guide will walk you through everything you need to know to get started with Midjourney in 2026, from creating your first image to mastering advanced prompting techniques that will help you produce exactly the visuals you envision.

## Getting Started with Midjourney

Before you can start creating images, you need to set up your account. Midjourney operates through Discord, a popular messaging platform. If you do not already have a Discord account, head to discord.com and create one — it is free and takes just a couple of minutes.

Once you have your Discord account ready, visit midjourney.com and click the "Join the Beta" button. This will invite you to the official Midjourney Discord server. After joining, you can start generating images immediately by navigating to one of the "newbies" channels. These channels are specifically designed for new users to experiment with the tool without feeling overwhelmed.

Midjourney offers several subscription tiers. The Basic plan provides roughly 200 image generations per month, which is enough for casual exploration. The Standard plan offers unlimited relaxed-mode generations and is the most popular choice for regular users. The Pro and Mega plans cater to professional users who need fast generation speeds and additional features like stealth mode, which keeps your images private.

## Your First Image: Basic Prompting

Creating your first image with Midjourney is remarkably simple. In any Midjourney-enabled Discord channel, type the `/imagine` command followed by your text description, called a "prompt." For example, you might type:

`/imagine a serene mountain lake at sunset with pine trees reflecting in still water`

After submitting your prompt, Midjourney will generate four image variations based on your description. This initial grid of four images typically takes 30 to 60 seconds to produce. Once the grid appears, you will see buttons labeled U1 through U4 and V1 through V4 beneath it. The U buttons upscale individual images to higher resolution, while the V buttons create new variations based on a specific image you liked.

Start with simple, descriptive prompts and observe the results. Pay attention to which words and phrases produce the most appealing outcomes. Even basic prompts like "a cozy coffee shop in autumn" or "a futuristic cityscape at night" can yield beautiful results. The key at this stage is experimentation — the more prompts you try, the better you will understand how Midjourney interprets your descriptions.

## Understanding Midjourney Parameters

Parameters are special commands you add to the end of your prompt to control various aspects of the image generation process. Mastering parameters is essential for getting consistent, high-quality results. Here are the most important parameters every user should know.

The aspect ratio parameter `--ar` controls the dimensions of your image. By default, Midjourney generates square images with a 1:1 ratio. For landscape images, use `--ar 16:9` or `--ar 3:2`. For portrait orientation, try `--ar 9:16` or `--ar 2:3`. For social media posts, `--ar 4:5` works well for Instagram, while `--ar 16:9` is ideal for YouTube thumbnails and blog headers.

The quality parameter `--q` adjusts the rendering quality and detail level. Values range from 0.25 to 2. Higher quality values produce more detailed images but take longer to generate and consume more GPU minutes. For initial exploration and drafting, `--q 0.5` is efficient. For final images you plan to use, `--q 2` provides maximum detail.

The stylize parameter `--s` controls how strongly Midjourney applies its artistic interpretation to your prompt. Lower values like `--s 50` produce images that more closely match your literal description. Higher values like `--s 750` give Midjourney more creative freedom, resulting in more artistic and stylized output. The default value is 100, which provides a good balance for most use cases.

The chaos parameter `--c` determines how varied the four initial images will be from each other. A low chaos value produces four similar images, while a high value like `--c 100` generates four wildly different interpretations. Use low chaos when you have a clear vision of what you want, and high chaos when you are brainstorming and exploring creative possibilities.

## Writing Effective Prompts

The quality of your prompts directly determines the quality of your output. Effective Midjourney prompts are specific, descriptive, and well-structured. Here are key principles for writing prompts that consistently produce excellent results.

First, be specific about your subject. Instead of "a dog," write "a golden retriever puppy sitting in a field of wildflowers." The more detail you provide about your subject, the more control you have over the outcome. Include information about the subject's appearance, pose, expression, and setting.

Second, describe the artistic style you want. Midjourney can emulate countless artistic styles. Try adding phrases like "oil painting style," "watercolor illustration," "photorealistic," "digital concept art," "anime style," "minimalist vector art," or "vintage photograph." You can even reference specific artists — "in the style of Studio Ghibli" or "reminiscent of Art Nouveau" — to guide the aesthetic direction.

Third, specify lighting and atmosphere. Lighting dramatically affects the mood of an image. Terms like "golden hour lighting," "dramatic backlighting," "soft diffused light," "neon glow," "candlelit," or "overcast moody lighting" can transform the entire feel of your generated image. Combine lighting descriptions with atmospheric terms like "misty," "ethereal," "gritty," or "dreamlike" for even more control.

Fourth, include technical camera terms for photorealistic images. If you want your image to look like a photograph, include terms like "shot on Canon EOS R5," "85mm lens," "shallow depth of field," "bokeh background," or "macro photography." These terms help Midjourney understand the photographic qualities you are looking for.

## Advanced Techniques

Once you are comfortable with basic prompting and parameters, several advanced techniques can dramatically improve your results and creative control.

Image prompting allows you to use an existing image as a reference. Upload an image to Discord and include its URL at the beginning of your prompt. Midjourney will use the image as inspiration for the new generation. This is incredibly useful for maintaining visual consistency across a series of images, creating variations of a concept you like, or blending the style of one image with a new subject.

Multi-prompts use the double colon separator `::` to give different parts of your prompt different weights. For example, `hot dog` generates an image of the food, but `hot:: dog` generates an image of a dog that is overheated. You can also assign numerical weights: `mountain landscape::2 autumn colors::1` tells Midjourney to emphasize the mountain landscape twice as much as the autumn colors.

Negative prompting with the `--no` parameter lets you exclude specific elements from your image. If you want a landscape without people, add `--no people, humans, figures` to your prompt. This is particularly useful for removing common unwanted elements like text, watermarks, or specific objects that Midjourney might otherwise include.

The remix mode, activated with `/settings` and toggling Remix Mode, allows you to modify your prompt when creating variations. Instead of simply generating similar images, you can adjust the prompt for each variation, enabling iterative refinement of your creative vision while maintaining elements you like from previous generations.

## Tips and Best Practices

After generating thousands of images with Midjourney, here are the best practices that will help you get the most out of the tool.

Start broad, then refine. Begin with a simple prompt to see Midjourney's initial interpretation, then gradually add more specific details in subsequent generations. This iterative approach is more efficient than trying to craft the perfect prompt on your first attempt.

Build a personal prompt library. When you find prompt structures or phrases that consistently produce great results, save them for future reference. Over time, you will develop a collection of reliable prompt templates that you can adapt for different projects.

Use the community for inspiration. The Midjourney Discord community showcases millions of images. Browse the showcase channels to discover prompt techniques and styles you might not have considered. You can also use the `/describe` command to upload an image and have Midjourney suggest prompts that would produce a similar result — this is an excellent learning tool.

Pay attention to composition. Add compositional terms to your prompts such as "rule of thirds," "centered composition," "bird's eye view," "close-up," or "wide establishing shot." These terms help Midjourney understand not just what you want in the image but how you want it arranged.

Experiment with combining unlikely elements. Some of the most striking Midjourney images come from unexpected combinations — "a library inside a whale," "a garden growing on a vintage typewriter," or "a city made of crystals." Do not be afraid to push creative boundaries and see what the AI produces.
